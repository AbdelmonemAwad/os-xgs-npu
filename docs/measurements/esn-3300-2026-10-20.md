# Extended sequence numbers arriving on the XGS 3300's engine. Measured, 2026-10-20

Yes. With this driver an association with extended sequence numbers that arrives is decrypted by
the XGS 3300's engine and holds: 20.7 million frames on one association at the link's rate, through
the sequence that killed it on 2026-10-12, through three minutes of 1,500-byte frames, and through a
rekey in the middle of a download - and no drop or authentication counter of the coprocessor moved.
So the driver takes it by default now, as the XGS 136's does, and both directions of an ESN tunnel
run on the engine.

On 2026-10-12 it did not hold ([under ESN, what arrives stays with the
kernel](../findings/esn-inbound-stays-with-the-kernel.md)): modules `c92ca5dd` and `a4c1091e`, a peer
that was another firewall, and the coprocessor decrypted a million frames or so and then refused every
one with `CRYPTO_DROP_AUTH` until a rekey, three runs in four. That cause was not found then and is
not found now; it is not there with this driver.

## What the driver does now

[octep_ipsec.c](../../contrib/octep/octep_ipsec.c) takes an arriving association marked
`SADB_X_SAFLAGS_ESN` and sends it with `ext_seq` (bit 18 of SA_ADD's control word), as it always did
for one that leaves. `dev.octep.0.ipsec.esn_decrypt` (tunable `hw.octep.ipsec_esn_decrypt`) at 0 leaves
it to the kernel as before, counted in `esn_refused`; it is read when an association is installed,
because the kernel offers one only then. OPNsense's IPsec pages have no ESN field: strongSwan takes it
through the ESP proposal (`aes256gcm16-esn`), set in `swanctl.conf` by hand at both ends.

## How it was measured

The lab tunnel between the second XGS 3300 and the XGS 136 behind its Port8, AES-GCM-16-256, the PC's
10G port on the 3300's side - so the PC sending to the 136 is the 3300 encrypting, and the 136 sending
to the PC is the 3300 decrypting. `images/deploy/lab-3300-esn.sh` (not in this repository) makes both
ends' `swanctl.conf` again from the GUI's configuration, puts the proposal and the 3300's replay window
in, ends the whole IKE association and has the child made again; then the 2026-10-12 sequence - four
streams up 16 s, four down 16 s, one down 12 s - with iperf3, and the 3300's engine counters.

**First, a rebooted peer.** The evening's first runs did die: after the child had been made again about
a dozen times, nothing the XGS 136 sent through the tunnel arrived at all - with the plain proposal too,
and with the 3300's kernel decrypting instead of its engine - and a reboot of the 136 cured it
([644](https://github.com/AbdelmonemAwad/os-xgs-npu/issues/644)). Every measurement below was made from a
rebooted 136, and the photographed one from both appliances rebooted.

## Measured

On module `d4fb3b12` (the switch at 1 by hand), one association, a 32-packet window, the 136 rebooted:

| | |
|---|---|
| the 2026-10-12 sequence, five times | up 984 to 985 Mbit/s, four down 983 to 985, one down 959 to 985 - every run |
| three minutes down, four streams, jumbo frames (the link is at MTU 9,000) | 984 Mbit/s every minute, 20.6 GB |
| three minutes down, four streams, 1,500-byte frames (the 136's Port8 held at 1,500 for it) | 905 to 907 Mbit/s, 19.0 GB, 15,188,960 frames decrypted by the engine |
| on that association in all | 20.7 million frames decrypted; the coprocessor's counters: `FROM_WIRE_TO_IPSEC_DECR` moving with them, no `CRYPTO_DROP` and no `AUTH` counter at all |
| a rekey 20 s into a 50 s four-stream download | 985, 976, 985, 982, 985 Mbit/s in its ten-second steps; the new arriving association on the engine |

A 4,096-packet window was tried too, before the peer was rebooted, and died as the 32 did then - which,
with the 136 in the state above, says nothing either way about the window.

Then the default, `f7eede10`: both appliances rebooted, no tunable set, `esn_decrypt` reading 1:

## What it does not mean

- **The 2026-10-12 failure is not explained**, only not reproduced: a different peer, a different
  module, and a driver much changed since (the replay window and its head, the second acknowledge,
  the slot for both directions). If it came from the coprocessor's firmware under a condition this
  bench does not make, it could come back; `esn_decrypt` at 0 puts the kernel back.
- **One peer, one appliance.** The second XGS 3300 and the XGS 136; the first XGS 3300 not tried.
- **A gigabit.** The tunnel runs over the 136's gigabit Port8; the 3300's engine at 10 Gbit/s with ESN is
  not measured.
