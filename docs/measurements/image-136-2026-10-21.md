# An image from the builder on the XGS 136: installed, updated, its configuration restored. 2026-10-21

The XGS 136 was installed clean from an image of [the image builder](https://build.npuforge.org/)
and used as an owner would use it: first boot, then an update of OPNsense from the GUI, then the
configuration taken before the reinstall restored. It is the second appliance an image from the
builder has been installed on, after an XGS 3300 on 2026-10-16, and the first of the ARMADA family.

![The XGS 136's own record: the release the image installed, the driver built at the first boot from
the sources the image carries, the update to 26.7.7 with the reboot held until the driver was built
for the new kernel, and the IPsec offload, ports and route after the restored configuration](../images/measurements/image-136-2026-10-21/01-installed-updated-restored.png)

## The image

- Built on the builder's page on 2026-10-21 at 18:31 UTC: OPNsense **26.7**, **serial** console at
  **115200**, with os-xgs-npu **v0.1.0-alpha.5.2** (`69372f5`) and the plugins.
- `/usr/local/share/os-xgs-npu/installed.json` on the appliance: `source: image`,
  `release: v0.1.0-alpha.5.2`.

## First boot

- **The front ports came up at the first boot**, with no network needed for it: Port1, the WAN, took
  its address by DHCP, the LAN bridge answered at `192.168.1.1`, and the console
  menu listed every port by its label on the chassis.
- **The driver was built at the first boot** from the kernel sources the image carries
  (`kernel-sources/src-12334a596709.tar.gz`, for OPNsense 26.7's kernel `stable/26.7-n283674-12334a596709`):
  `built 278120 bytes from 9 sources` in `install-build.log`.

## The update, as an owner does it

The owner pressed Update in System: Firmware, which took OPNsense from 26.7 to **26.7.7** and the
kernel from `12334a596709` to `abbbe67a45e7`. Watched from outside, reading only
(`images/deploy/watch-update.sh`), and from the appliance's own log afterwards:

| UTC | what |
|---|---|
| 19:12:57 | the update starts (`opnsense-update`) |
| 19:14:38 | the new kernel is on disk |
| 19:15:01 | the five-minute tick of the kernel follower finds it, and starts fetching its sources over the WAN |
| 19:15:02 | the update asks for its reboot; the stop hook (`rc.syshook.d/stop/20-octep`) **holds it** |
| 19:15:50 | the sources are in; the build starts |
| 19:15:54 | the driver built and installed for the new kernel, the one before kept as `npuep.ko.prev` |
| 19:15:56 | the stop hook finds the driver matching the next boot's kernel, and the reboot goes on |

The reboot waited 54 seconds. The appliance came back on the new kernel with the driver attached,
the WAN up and the internet reachable. Nothing was done by hand.

## The configuration restored

The configuration taken just before the reinstall - every interface, the lab's addresses and
routes, the certificate, SSH, the XGS settings and the plugins' - was put back as System:
Configuration: Backups does it, and the appliance rebooted.

- **Back at the first boot:** the addresses of the lab's links on Port8 and F1, the
  Let's Encrypt certificate of the web GUI, SSH, and the XGS settings applied while running:
  automatic acceleration on (`dev.npuep.0.flow.auto: 1`), the anomaly watch running.
- **Back at the second boot only:** the settings the driver reads when it attaches - IPsec offload
  and tunnel flows. OPNsense writes them into `/boot/loader.conf` during the boot, after the loader
  has read the file before it. After the second boot: decrypt 1, encrypt 1, tunnel flows 1
  ([669](https://github.com/AbdelmonemAwad/os-xgs-npu/issues/669)). **Fixed the same evening:** the
  early hooks now put the configuration's values into the kernel environment just before they load
  the driver, so they are taken at the first boot - measured on this XGS 136 and on the XGS 3300,
  each booted with the driver's lines taken out of `/boot/loader.conf` as a restore leaves it.
- **Not in the configuration**, so new after a reinstall whatever is restored: the appliance's
  anonymous identity (`/var/db/os-xgs-npu/appliance-id`: counted on the site as a new appliance), the
  SSH host keys (`/conf/sshd`: a client that knew the appliance warns of a changed key), the problem
  reports, and the host's key for the management link (`/root/npu/id_ed25519_npu`).
- A plugin installed from OPNsense's own catalogue (here `os-acme-client`) is named in the
  configuration's plugin list and is not installed by a restore.

## Found on the way

- **The management link was never trusted on an image install**
  ([668](https://github.com/AbdelmonemAwad/os-xgs-npu/issues/668)). Putting the host's key on the
  coprocessor had been a step done once by hand, so every read of the coprocessor - PoE, the
  transceivers, health - went over its 115200 console one at a time, and the Ports page's PoE card
  showed "the coprocessor's console is busy with another command". A fix is on that issue, tested
  on this appliance.
- **XGS: Ports' errors and drops read "Not available on this appliance"** on the XGS 136 by design
  since 2026-10-20: reading its switch makes the coprocessor's network agent report linked ports
  down ([639](https://github.com/AbdelmonemAwad/os-xgs-npu/issues/639)).
