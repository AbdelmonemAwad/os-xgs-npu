# IPsec associations on SFOS 23's coprocessor software: refused, then taken, 2026-10-25

Found while starting on discovery 14 ([IPsec state kept in step](../findings/discoveries-2026-10-22.md#ipsec-state-kept-in-step)):
on the first XGS 3300, whose coprocessor runs SFOS 23 EAP 1's software, **no association had been
offloaded since it came back to OPNsense**. Its lab tunnel to the second XGS 3300 - the PC's network
to the first board's own address, IKEv2, AES-GCM-256 - ran in the kernel's cipher, and the driver
said why at every install: the coprocessor refused the association, rc 1, `SP2FP_RC_INVALID_PARAMETER`.

SFOS 23 lengthened `SA_ADD`'s request. Its host's `fw_fp_add_egress_sa` zeroes and sends 0xc8 bytes
where SFOS 21's sends 0xc0, and its `populate_add_req` stores every field from the key on 8 bytes
further - the window at 0x78 instead of 0x70, the SPI at 0x7c instead of 0x74, the sequence at 0x80,
the revision at 0xc0 - with a word at 8, taken from the kernel's `xfrm_state`, and four bytes of
padding before them. The far side refuses a request shorter than its own, so every 192-byte request
was refused ([rpc-numbering.md](../families/rpc-numbering.md#sa_adds-layout)). The other IPsec
commands kept their sizes. Both drivers now lay `SA_ADD` out by the version - 192 bytes on SFOS 21
and 22, 200 on 23 with the word sent 0, as SFOS 23's host sends it for a tunnel that is not
route-based - and send no association while the version is not known (`sfosnum_sa_add_shift`).
octep.ko `4c844f00` on both XGS 3300s, npuep.ko `590205fb` on the XGS 136
(`images/deploy/lab-sa23.sh`).

It is not: it counts the offers the driver itself declined - mostly another interface's association,

The other two boards kept theirs with the same build: the second XGS 3300 (SFOS 22) its four, the
XGS 136 (SFOS 21) its two, accepted by the engine.

## A stream through it

TCP from the PC to the first board through the tunnel - the second board encrypting, the first
decrypting and delivering to its own host - and back the other way, read by hand after the
pictures, not photographed:

| | runs | Mbit/s |
|---|---|---|
| into the first board (its engine decrypts) | 5, of 6 to 10 s | 343, 1,770, 967, 196, 231 |
| out of it (its engine encrypts) | 3, of 6 to 10 s | 3,190, 3,580, 3,370 |

In the 1,770 Mbit/s run the first board's engine decrypted 152,032 frames and its fast path counted
no drop, nor did the driver's receive path (`rx_bad`, `rx_nokey`, `rx_queuefail` unmoved); the
counters that moved are the ones the SFOS 23 list names `RX_IPSEC`, `FROM_WIRE_TO_IPSEC_DECR` and
`FROM_KN_TO_IPSEC_ENCR` - 152,032, 152,032 and 152,041.

## What it does not say

- Why the stream into the first board varies from 196 to 1,770 Mbit/s while the other direction
  holds at 3.2 to 3.6 Gbit/s. It was not compared with SFOS 22's software on the same path: the
  path into the second board's own host through the tunnel is not open in the lab's rules.
- The ARMADA driver's SFOS 23 layout is the same table's, built and run on SFOS 21 only: no ARMADA
  board here runs SFOS 23's software. The XGS 126 of #736 does.
- What the word at 8 does for a route-based tunnel.
